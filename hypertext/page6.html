— sometime after —

You wonder if the choice mattered.

Not whether it worked out — you know how it worked out, or you don't yet, and either way the knowing feels strangely separate from the question. What you're wondering is something harder to name. Whether the sending was different from the not-sending. Whether the waiting changed anything, or just felt like it did.

Maybe all the small hesitations add up to something. Maybe they don't. Maybe that's the same thing.

You think about the moment before you decided. How it felt weighted. How you stood there in the kitchen or on the couch or wherever you were, holding the phone, and something in you treated it like a threshold. As if the right choice existed and you were trying to locate it.

But choices aren't places. They're just what you do next.

You did something next. It became the past. The past doesn't ask to be justified, mostly — it just sits there, settled, already having happened, no longer editable.

There is another message now, from someone else, unopened.

You hold the phone and start over.
