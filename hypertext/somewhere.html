— somewhere —

The phone is on a table.

Or in a pocket. Or face-down, the way phones sometimes are when their owner has decided not to look at them for a while, or has just forgotten to.

The screen lights up briefly. The notification slides into view, summarized into one short line, and then dims again into the long list of other things that are technically happening to this person without their attention.

It is small, on the screen.

Smaller than it was when you sent it. The way a thing always looks smaller from the outside than from the inside.

There is a kettle somewhere, or a TV, or the sound of someone else in the next room. There is a meal in the middle of being eaten, or a conversation that didn't pause when the phone lit up. The room the message arrived in does not know it arrived. The hour is whatever hour it happens to be in this room. It is not 11:47 pm here. It might not even be the same day.

The notification will be seen, eventually. Or it will be missed and seen later, mixed in with twenty others. Or it will be opened and closed without a reply, the way many things are. None of those outcomes is happening yet.

What is happening is this: the phone is on the table, and the message is sitting on top of it, waiting the way only delivered things wait — already arrived, not yet arrived at.

You go back.
