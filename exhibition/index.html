Curatorial Statement

This exhibition brings together three net art works that explore what happens when online spaces fall apart. Each piece uses the web's own structure — frames, code, page layouts — to show how fragile digital spaces really are.

Olia Lialina's My Boyfriend Came Back From the War (1996) is a story told through clicking. Each click splits the browser into smaller and smaller frames, breaking apart a conversation between two people until there's almost nothing left. JODI's wwwwwwwww.jodi.org (1995) throws out everything we expect from a website — no navigation, no clean design — and instead dumps raw, messy code onto the screen. And Lialina and Dragan Espenschied's One Terabyte of Kilobyte Age (2010–ongoing) saves and shares screenshots of nearly 400,000 GeoCities homepages that were wiped out when Yahoo shut the platform down in 2009.

What ties these works together is more than just a theme of digital loss. All three use some form of structural fragmentation — splitting frames, exposing source code, archiving deleted pages — as a way to make the web's instability something you can actually see and feel. Spanning from 1995 to the present, they show how net artists have been grappling with digital decay across the entire history of the web.

01 / 03

My Boyfriend Came Back From the War

Olia Lialina · 1996

A browser-based hypertext narrative where clicking on images and text splits the screen into smaller and smaller frames, revealing a fragmented story about a couple reuniting after war. Each click fractures the conversation further — the interface itself becomes a metaphor for the impossibility of fully reconnecting.

02 / 03

wwwwwwwww.jodi.org

JODI (Joan Heemskerk & Dirk Paesmans) · 1995

An early net art piece consisting of chaotic, maze-like HTML pages filled with raw code, glitches, and dead ends. JODI subverts every convention of web design — stripping away usability to expose the messy, unstable code that hides beneath clean interfaces. Navigation becomes disorientation; the browser becomes hostile territory.

03 / 03

One Terabyte of Kilobyte Age

Olia Lialina & Dragan Espenschied · 2010–ongoing

An ongoing archival project that restores and shares screenshots of nearly 400,000 GeoCities homepages saved before Yahoo deleted the platform in 2009. Each recovered page is a fragment of someone's self-expression — guestbooks, "under construction" banners, tiled backgrounds — now preserved as artifacts of a web that no longer exists.

Thematic Connections
01 — Structural Fragmentation as Aesthetic Strategy

All three works use the web's own structural elements — frames, HTML markup, page layouts — as raw material. Lialina fractures the browser into nesting frames. JODI exposes and corrupts the underlying code. Lialina and Espenschied reconstruct a grid of pages that were erased. In each case, the form of the web becomes the content of the art.

02 — Connection and Its Collapse

Each work engages with the promise of digital connection and its failure. A couple tries and fails to reconnect through a disintegrating interface. A website refuses to function as a website. An entire platform of personal expression is wiped clean. Together, they argue that the web's infrastructure of togetherness is also, always, an infrastructure of loss.

03 — Across the Web's History

Spanning 1995 to the present, these works trace how net artists have grappled with digital decay across the web's lifespan. From the early experiments of the mid-90s, when the web was still new enough to be broken open, to the archival urgency of the 2010s — they document a consistent artistic practice of sitting with what falls apart.
